# Suppress instance tint and place instances in every mask pass

Every rendered gate metric is read back out of a pass that replaces each renderable's material with an encoded one and reads the framebuffer. three.js multiplies a material's colour by an `InstancedMesh`'s `instanceColor` whenever one is present, whatever material is bound, so for any instanced mesh carrying a per-instance colour the identity a pass wrote was not the identity that reached the framebuffer. The reference's two large ground-rock populations call `setColorAt` and its three grass populations do not. Measured on the authored overview, `cover`'s reference mask held 128 pixels while the scene draws 5,299, and all 5,172 missing pixels were credited to `plazas`, whose mask was therefore 84 per cent scatter: `cover` is identity 13, which needs 260 of 255 and clamps, and the authored warm-grey tint of about 0.55 red carries the clamped value onto identity 7. The same defect ran through the silhouette, semantic-group, and Material-Family passes and through the inventory's own visibility pass, on the reference side only, because the candidate's cover carries no per-instance colour. Ticket 09 spent a round fitting the candidate's scatter against a reference number that was wrong by a factor of forty-one, and its recorded diagnosis — that the candidate was seventeen times too visible — was the exact inverse of the truth.

Two further encoding defects sat alongside it and are corrected together, because any one of them invalidates every stored capture and re-measuring once is cheaper than three times. The depth and world-normal passes are `ShaderMaterial`s, so no three.js chunk applied `instanceMatrix` for them and every instance of every population rendered stacked on its mesh's own origin; depth and orientation evidence for an instanced group measured whatever stood behind it. And the identity lattice's step of 20 could not carry the fourteenth declared group at all, so `environment` clamped onto `cover`'s value.

The encodings move to `tools/evaluation/scene-pass-encoding.mjs`, which imports nothing but `three`, so `test/scene-pass-encoding.test.mjs` holds them to analytical fixtures the way the metric functions already are: every declared identity round-trips through the byte it is written into, a pass suppresses per-instance colour and restores it even when the render throws, and both shaders place and turn instanced geometry. Four of its six assertions fail against the previous behaviour. This is what was missing — the passes had no check of their own, so an encoding defect and a candidate defect were indistinguishable in the reported numbers, which is the same shape as the four harness defects the fixed-camera calibration itself found.

The corrected masks then exposed a saturated metric, and it is repaired the way ADR-0051 repaired the last one. With cover's real mask in place, a *mild* control fails the frozen worst-group silhouette threshold: a 0.02-radian yaw of the reference against itself takes cover's IoU to 0.0415 and a one per cent scale takes it to 0.1344, against 0.302569. Cover is 5,100 instances one to a few pixels across, so once anything moves further than an instance's own footprint there is no intersection left, and a maximum over every camera and group therefore reports cover as the scene's worst failure whatever the island looks like. That is the same failure as the per-pixel world-normal saturation ADR-0051 found, and it takes the same remedy: fix what the metric compares, not what it demands. A per-pixel intersection *is* an instance pairing, and Distributed Scene Cover is defined as compared "by semantic occupancy and spatial distribution rather than arbitrary instance pairing", so the worst-group intersection ranges over groups whose members carry individual identity.

Only the intersection is scoped, and the scope is bounded by a fixture rather than by intent, because excluding a group from a worst-case metric is one keystroke away from hiding an unfavourable result. Cover stays in every mean, stays in the contour-distance and depth maxima, and its own intersection is still reported as `worstDistributed`. Those two maxima keep it genuinely gated because their brackets separate on it: cover's contour distance runs from 17.9 pixels under the worst mild control to 268.6 under a severe one, and its depth from 5.3 world units to 86.5. The candidate's cover contour distance is 223.9 pixels against a threshold of 39.3, so cover cannot pass while it is wrong. The scoping also made the metric harder, not easier — with cover out of the maximum the bracket is `paths` at 0.4299 mild against `vegetation` at 0.1872 severe, and the threshold rises from 0.302569 to 0.369228.

The thresholds are re-derived rather than adjusted. The reference is on both sides of every calibration control, so a mismeasured reference mask moved the bracket as well as the candidate, and the frozen numbers were calibrated through the defective encoding. All sixteen reference-only controls are re-captured under the corrected passes and the baseline advances to a named revision, with no threshold chosen and no candidate result consulted: the selection rule, the controls, and the damage magnitudes are unchanged, and only what the passes measure has changed. Twelve of the fourteen rendered thresholds move and `scene-quality-baseline-v1.2` lists each with both values, asserted against the file on disk by the same rule that already forbids an undeclared move in the world-space layers. Two of them get stricter — worst group silhouette IoU from 0.302569 to 0.369228 and worst semantic confusion from 0.007092 to 0.006534 — because their previous brackets were measured through a reference whose scatter had been credited to `plazas`. Three loosen because the corrected passes measure more than the defective ones did: cover's world normals are rendered at all now, and cover's own rows join the depth and intersection means. The four appearance moves are in the fourth decimal place. One metric stays demoted, the same one ADR-0051 demoted, for the same measured reason.

Nothing here loosens a threshold to accommodate a candidate. The direction of travel is the evidence for that: the two worst-case metrics a correct mask makes discriminating again both tightened, and the reference's `cover` mask is now forty-one times larger than the candidate has to fill.
