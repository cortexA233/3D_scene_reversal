# Record the terrain landform budget as a count boundary

The frozen terrain gates require height p95 within 5.26875 over the full probe field and within 2.85035 over the shore band, and the Bounded Semantic Terrain Program's budget of 40 named analytic landforms cannot reach either. Measured by running the production landform pursuit at increasing budgets against the same frozen elevation evidence acceptance reads, 40 forms reach 8.657, 60 reach 7.492, 80 reach 5.937, 120 reach 4.673, 160 reach 4.198 and 320 reach 3.004. Interpolating the two rows that straddle it, the full gate is crossed at about **101 landforms against a frozen budget of 40**. The shore gate is not crossed by any budget tested: shore p95 flattens at 4.976 by 160 forms and 4.121 by 320, improving 0.385 per 0.42 doublings, so reaching 2.85035 by adding forms would take thousands.

Each of those rows is an upper bound, because matching pursuit is greedy, so on its own the curve cannot distinguish a budget that is too small from a fitter that is not good enough. The second measurement settles that from the other side. The program's finest landform radius is 12 world units and its value noise is uncorrelated with the reference, so noise adds error as often as it removes it and detail below 12 units is unreachable by any fit. That detail's own p95 is **2.428** over the full field, 2.617 over land and 1.909 over the shore band — comfortably *under* both thresholds. The landform family is therefore adequate and the count is what is short. This is a count boundary, not a representation-family boundary, and the two call for different responses.

The budget is not grown. The spec's instruction is explicit — "If the fitted program cannot reach the threshold within it, that is a representation-boundary result to be recorded, not a budget increase" — and the reason survives inspection: 101 two-dimensional landforms at roughly six controls each is around 600 numbers to describe a 300-unit-radius island, which is a sampled elevation field wearing a generator's name and is what the frozen budget exists to prevent. The threshold is not touched either. It is honestly calibrated from reference-only perturbations and it is the right thing to ask of a terrain; it is simply not reachable by 40 analytic forms.

Two in-budget refinements were measured and are recorded rather than taken, because neither closes the gap and the first was proposed for the wrong reason. The reference carries an azimuthally-continuous coastal ring about two units above its own interior plateau, cresting at 0.82 of the coastal radius, and the program's shelf is a monotone ramp that cannot rise above `groundY` — worth about two units of systematic bias against a 3.3-unit gap on the full gate and 5.1 on the shore gate. And an earlier note in the ticket proposed per-node azimuthal shore controls; measured over 24 azimuths the shore band's signed bias runs -4.9 to +6.6 while its absolute p95 is 8.89, so the per-azimuth term is the smaller one and the radial shape is the larger.

The shore gate has a separate cause worth naming, because it is not the budget. Scales finer than the base radius are refused beyond normalised coastal radius 0.8, a constraint added in an earlier round because letting them in pushed coastline symmetric p95 from 15.75 to 27.85 past its 22.03 threshold. That constraint is correct and it protects a real gate, but it leaves the shore band with nothing but the three-number shelf, which is why shore p95 has a floor that landforms do not remove. A rim whose contribution is zero at the shoreline cannot move the coastline by construction, so there is a way to give the shore band shape without breaking the gate the constraint defends. It is named here as the next attempt inside the budget rather than claimed as a fix.

The terrain checks that assert these two thresholds are marked outstanding with this record as their reason, unchanged and not loosened, in the same form ADR-0052 used for the horizon form budget. `tools/development/measure-terrain-form-budget.mjs` reproduces every number above.
