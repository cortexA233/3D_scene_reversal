# Give the surface sample budget to the entity

Scene Surface Parity compares two point sets, so how those points are spread over each subject decides what the metric can see. The budget was allocated per mesh — `min(96, max(12, sqrt(triangles) * 3))` for each — and that formula is sub-linear and floored, so a small part draws far more points than its share of the geometry. The metric therefore depended on how a form is divided into parts, and it penalised exactly the semantic part structure a generator is required to have.

Measured, a generated bamboo clump that separates culms from foliage gave its culms 28 per cent of its samples for 13 per cent of its triangles, while the authored placement is a single mesh whose culms are about three parts in a thousand and were never sampled at all. Split by direction, `bamboo` read 4.12 reference-to-candidate against 39.32 the other way, a ratio of 9.55, while `palm` read 0.81 and `blossom` 1.15. The candidate's canopy already covered the authored one; the entire penalty came from how the candidate was divided, and `bamboo` was the largest single contributor to the failing surface gate. The same mechanism distorted every development measurement built on the sampler: a building's new thin plinth drew 25.9 per cent of its samples in the base decile where the authored building has 4.6, and single-placement kinds carry 96 samples, which is enough for the sign of a taper and nothing finer.

An entity's budget now comes from its *total* triangle count and is split across its meshes in proportion, with the rounding remainder going to the largest parts so the total is exact and any mesh with triangles getting at least one point. A single-mesh subject is sampled exactly as before, which matters because the authored placements are overwhelmingly one mesh each; a multi-part subject is sampled as its own merged equivalent would be, which is what makes the two subjects comparable at all. `test/surface-sampling.test.mjs` holds that invariance to fixtures — splitting a body in two may not change how many points describe it, and a part's share of the samples must be its share of the triangles.

The rule also stopped being duplicated. The reference measurement carried its own copy of the same formula and applied it per *renderable*, so an authored placement made of five meshes drew up to five budgets while one made of a single mesh drew one. Both sides now import one implementation, and the reference groups its renderables by placement before allocating. Two copies of a sampling rule is how the reference and the candidate came to be sampled differently while both formulas read identically.

The world-geometry thresholds are re-derived rather than adjusted. The reference is on both sides of every world-space control, so a change in how it is sampled moves the bracket as well as the candidate, and the previous numbers were calibrated through the per-mesh allocation. Nothing is chosen and no candidate result is consulted: the controls, their damage magnitudes, and the selection rule are unchanged, and only the distribution of the points they are measured over has changed. The baseline advances to a named revision that lists every moved threshold with both values, asserted against the file on disk.

This is the third frozen measurement this milestone has had to repair — after the mask-pass encodings of ADR-0053 and the invented population controls of ticket 09 — and all three had the same shape: a measurement that looked like a candidate result. The fixtures added alongside each are the actual mitigation, because a defect in a measurement is invisible in the number it produces.
